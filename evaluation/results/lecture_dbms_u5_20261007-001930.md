## Contradictions with the notes (spot-check these)

- **Recovery, Checkpoints, and Distributed Commit Protocols** — "In the prepare phase each server writes a “prepare‑ok” entry to its own log and replies yes; if the crash happens before the coordinator can broadcast the final COMMIT, both servers will, upon restart, look at their logs, see the prepare record but no commit, and automatically roll back the tentative changes, guaranteeing atomicity across sites." — judge: The notes state that in Phase 2, if all participants reply Yes, the coordinator sends COMMIT. They do not state that servers automatically roll back if the commit is missing after a crash; standard 2PC recovery involves the coordinator checking the log to decide whether to commit or abort, not the participants independently rolling back based solely on a missing commit record (which could lead to inconsistency if the coordinator actually intended to commit).

## Errors on things the notes don't cover (judge's own knowledge — spot-check)

- **Fundamentals of Locking Mechanisms** — "Only after T2 writes and calls `unlock(A)` does the lock become free again, allowing any other transaction to proceed – and because each transaction obeyed the grow‑then‑shrink pattern, we’ve avoided lost updates, dirty reads, and even deadlocks." — judge: The sentence claims that following the grow-then-shrink pattern (2PL) avoids deadlocks. The notes explicitly state that Basic 2PL can result in deadlocks (showing a deadlock example) and that Conservative 2PL avoids deadlocks due to pre-locking, not the basic 2PL protocol itself. Additionally, 2PL does not prevent dirty reads (only Strict/Rigorous 2PL does).
